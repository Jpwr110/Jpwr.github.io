Internet security is a term that involves browser, network, application, and operating system security. There is a high risk of coming into contact with scams and viruses, as the internet is an insecure channel for sharing information. Common scams and viruses one might encounter include: phishing, downloadable viruses, trojan horses, worms, and spyware. Some of those options listed are designed to trick the user into downloading them. Often thinking that they are harmless or of great importance, the user will unknowingly invite harmful software onto their computer. Malware is short for malicious software. Malware is defined as any software used to disrupt computer operation, gather sensitive information, or gain access to private computer systems. A botnet is a network of zombie computers that have been taken over by a robot or bot that performs large-scale malicious acts for the creator of the botnet. There are several ways you as a computer user can prevent these sorts of things from happening to you, some of them are already in place right now. Multi-factor authentication, firewalls, antivirus, and a password manager are all used to help stop the threat of computer compromisation. A computer firewall controls access between networks and screens network traffic to block traffic that is dangerous. All modern computers come with built-in and pre-installed firewall software that is used to protect the internet user from online harm. The same can be said for antivirus software. Created by your computer manufacturer, the main goal of antivirus software is to prevent your computer from downloading viruses and stop them if they are downloaded. The objective of scams and viruses is (most of the time) to gain access to personal user information, for monetary purposes. The creators of these scams and viruses are looking to get their hands on your bank information, passwords, personal records, data, and other sensitive information.
