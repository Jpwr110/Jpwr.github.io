VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol) and IP (Internet Protocol) make voice communications over the internet possible. VoIP allows the user to make and receive phone calls using a broadband internet connection as opposed to traditional copper telephone lines. IP is what converts the spoken messages into internet data to be shared/transmitted. VoIP and IP technology can be used by common electronic devices such as smartphones, personal computers, and internet access devices. The technology is used to deliver phone calls, SMS messages, faxes, and voice messages. When large amounts of data are to be transmitted, they are broken up into smaller packets before being sent off, this process is called “packet switching” and is used by packet switching networks. For example, the internet is a package switching network. The File Transfer Protocol (FTP) is used to share files between the user and the server. FTP can be used to transfer local files on your own personal computer onto the web for other users to see and interact with. For clarification, FTP is not any type of program or software, but rather a method of moving files from one computer to another. Peer to Peer file sharing programs (P2P) are programs that allow users to share files with other users to download for free. The content shared on these P2P programs is often copyrighted material such as music, movies, TV shows, and videogames. The copyright holders of these files do not approve of sharing their content for free, and monitor these programs closely. If you are caught illegally downloading or sharing copyrighted material you can have your internet access revoked, taken to court, or even have to pay a hefty fine to the copyright holders. Most P2P programs automatically upload the files on your computer to be shared with other users. This includes personal image/video files, as well as personal documents and records. Putting your safety and personal information at risk to watch a movie for free will ultimately cost you more than if you just paid for the content legally.
